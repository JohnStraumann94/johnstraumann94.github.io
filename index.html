01 — ABOUT

AI should be useful, not merely impressive.

As a Chief AI Officer, my focus is on connecting emerging AI capabilities to real organizational outcomes. That means asking the hard questions: What problem are we solving? What changes operationally? What is the risk? And how do we know it worked?

The strongest AI programs combine technology leadership with disciplined execution — clear strategy, sound architecture, responsible deployment, and an organization prepared to use the technology effectively.

04 — PLAYBOOKS & TOOLKITS

Strategy you can actually run.

Two complementary bodies of work: governance blueprints for the leadership team, and the automation that stands up the platform underneath them.

AI PLAYBOOKS

Governance blueprints by industry

Four editions of a single governance framework. Each shares the same control spine — security, governance, identity, data privacy, Canada/US regulatory compliance, human oversight, incident response, vendor risk, data strategy, change management, and board-level metrics — and each closes with a First 90 Days roadmap and reusable governance artifacts.

  • Chief AI Officer Playbook The cross-industry core: an operating manual for the officer handed accountability for AI. Defines the mandate, the priority mandate portfolio, board reporting, and what to refuse. Executive summary available.
  • Banking AI Playbook Ranks the five departments with the greatest AI benefit — fraud & AML, contact center, credit risk and underwriting, regulatory reporting, and back-office operations — against a Canada/US regulatory map.
  • State & Local Government AI Playbook Targets the five programs best suited to early adoption — benefits eligibility, unemployment insurance, DMV and licensing, tax and revenue, and public safety and courts — with a dedicated chapter on accessibility, language access, and digital equity.
  • Automotive Dealer AI Playbook Covers the five highest-benefit dealership functions, from an AI deal desk and BDC engagement agent to fixed ops, inventory pricing, and F&I automation — bounded by management-set guardrails and a disclose-don't-disguise rule.

DEPLOYMENT AUTOMATION

PowerShell toolkit for AI infrastructure

The playbooks specify a common Azure reference architecture so every use case is built on the same pre-approved footing. These scripts provision it — repeatable, reviewable, and governed by default rather than assembled by hand in the portal.

  • Landing zone & infrastructure Segregated, policy-compliant environments with consistent naming, tagging, and region placement — with Azure Policy guardrails applied at provisioning time.
  • Data platform The governed, centralized data foundation that retrieval and analytics workloads depend on, wired to the right storage and access boundaries.
  • Model development & hosting Azure AI and OpenAI model deployments, retrieval and search indexes, and the quota and content-filtering configuration around them.
  • Identity, monitoring & guardrails Managed identities, key management, least-privilege RBAC, private endpoints, and diagnostic settings — so deployments are auditable from day one.
